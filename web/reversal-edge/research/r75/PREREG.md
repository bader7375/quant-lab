# Round 75 plan (written 2026-11-11 before the download): what did listed SAR cash-like funds really pay?

Every account number assumes idle money earns the US 13-week T-bill yield (the riyal is pegged to the dollar). Idle money is about
96% of the account, so this assumption carries a large part of the return. Yahoo lists two Saudi sukuk ETFs: 9404.SR (Alinma,
short-maturity government sukuk, from 2020) and 9403.SR (Albilad, sovereign sukuk, from 2022). Money-market mutual funds are not on Yahoo.

Descriptive only, no rule is tested. For each ETF, from its first bar: total return a year (adjusted close, so payouts count),
worst drawdown, worst calendar year, median value traded a day, against T-bill interest over the same dates.
Fixed before looking: a fund is called "a usable cash leg" here only if its return is within 0.5 point a year of the T-bill
assumption or above it, its worst drawdown is under 1%, and its median value traded is at least SAR 100,000 a day. Otherwise the
report says the assumption is not backed by a listed instrument and rests on money-market funds I cannot measure.
